Your videos, news and anime in one place, on your own computer.

Tubcal is a free app that gathers the YouTube channels, subreddits, Hacker News, code projects and anime you follow, and shows them without ads. It runs on your computer and keeps what you watch and save there.

Each part of Tubcal is shown below in a short video. Scroll through the list and the TV plays the part you're reading about, or click a title to play it.

Watching and reading

  1. Tubcal opens on a home screen shaped like a TV. It shows the video you stopped halfway through, new episodes of the shows you follow, what's live, and what's new from your channels. Click a picture and it plays.

  2. Search YouTube or pick something from your channels, and it plays inside Tubcal, without ads. Send the video to a corner of the screen and it keeps playing while you look around.

  3. Follow channels without a YouTube account. New uploads are sorted by day, you can look at one channel at a time or hide what you've seen, and Tubcal remembers where you stopped in each video.

  4. Connect a free AniList account and Tubcal shows the anime you're watching, which ones have new episodes, and when the next one airs. You can also browse each season, check the week's schedule and see everything you've watched.

  5. Choose videos for the Archive and Tubcal writes down what's said in them. Then ask a question in your own words. An AI running on your own computer answers and shows which video, and which minute, the answer came from. Click one to watch that moment.

  6. Each day Tubcal makes a front page from the channels, subreddits and Hacker News stories you follow. With the AI it writes a short piece about each top story; without it you get the headlines. Stories you've read are ticked off.

  7. Read Hacker News and the subreddits you follow, with the comments open next to the list. On Hacker News, Tubcal shows how fast each story is rising and which comments are new since you last looked.

  8. Videos, posts, stories, shows and projects all have a save button. Everything you save goes into one list that you can open from any page, search, tick off and export.

  9. Tubcal comes with eight looks, from an old wooden TV set to green text on a black screen. Pick one in Settings. You can also switch off the parts of Tubcal you don't use.

  10. With 3D objects switched on, the home screen becomes a box you can turn, with a channel on each side. Hacker News gets a machine that sorts the day's top stories into five topics.

For people who write code

  1. Open the projects on your computer, edit them with Vim keys, and run commands in a terminal under the code. Lines you've changed are marked in the margin.

  2. A page each for Rust, Python, Go and TypeScript, with talks, podcasts, release notes and the official manuals. A podcast keeps playing while you move around Tubcal.

  3. Follow projects and people on GitHub and see what changed: new releases, new issues and what's popular today. Open a project to read about it and look through its files. No GitHub account needed.

Other ways to use it

  1. Tubcal also runs inside a terminal window and is used with the keyboard. It shows pictures right in the terminal and plays videos in a separate window.

  2. Tubcal for Android puts the same rooms on your phone: the morning paper, your channels, your anime, Hacker News and Reddit, the Archive and your saved things. It runs on the phone itself.

Get Tubcal

Tubcal runs on Linux: Ubuntu, Fedora, Debian, Arch, openSUSE and most others. Installing it takes one line.

  1. Open a terminal

    Look for “Terminal” in your app menu.

  2. Paste this line and press Enter

    curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Shio-T0/Tubcal/main/install.sh | bash

  3. Choose the parts you want

    The installer lists Tubcal's parts and shows what it's going to install. Nothing happens until you agree. When it's done, open Tubcal from your app menu.

There's an Android app too. If your phone is plugged in with USB debugging switched on, the installer offers to put Tubcal on it.

Good to know

What it costs
Nothing. Tubcal is free and open source, under the GPL-3.0 licence.
Accounts
You don't need one for YouTube, Reddit, Hacker News or GitHub. The anime list uses a free AniList account.
Your data
Your channels, history and saved things are kept on your computer. Tubcal has no server of its own, so there's no Tubcal account. It only connects to the sites you use through it.
The AI parts
The Archive, the written stories in the morning paper and the topic sorting for Hacker News use an AI that runs on your own computer, through the free Ollama app. Everything else works without it.
